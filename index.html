Why Utah Homeowners Get Multiple Concrete Quotes

Concrete is one of those projects where the cheapest bid often costs the most in the long run. A driveway poured too thin, without rebar or wire mesh, or on a poorly compacted base will crack and heave within a few winters — and then you're paying to tear it out and do it twice. Getting two or three quotes lets you compare not just price, but thickness, reinforcement, base prep, and finish details.

Good questions to ask any concrete contractor: How thick will you pour the driveway? What reinforcement are you using? How are you prepping and compacting the base? Where will the control joints go? How long before I can drive on it? A contractor who answers these clearly is usually a contractor who does the work right.

Best time of year to pour concrete in Utah

Late spring through early fall is ideal — warm enough for proper curing, dry enough for steady scheduling. Winter pours are possible with blankets and additives, but they cost more and carry more risk. If your project can wait for May through October, you'll get better conditions and often better scheduling.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a concrete driveway cost in Salt Lake City?

It depends on size, thickness, site prep, and finish — a plain driveway costs far less per square foot than stamped or colored concrete. That's exactly why we recommend getting free quotes: describe your project once and let local pros price it for your specific situation. Quotes are free and there's no obligation.

How long before I can drive on new concrete?

Most contractors recommend waiting at least 7 days before driving on a new driveway, and a full 28 days before parking heavy vehicles on it. Foot traffic is usually fine after 24–48 hours. Your contractor will give you exact timing based on the mix and weather.

Will my concrete crack in Utah winters?

All concrete cracks eventually — the goal is controlling where. Proper control joints, adequate thickness, reinforcement, and a compacted base keep cracking to hairline joints instead of structural breaks. Freeze-thaw is Utah's biggest enemy, so sealing your concrete every few years makes a real difference.

Do I need a permit for a new driveway or patio?

It depends on your city and the project. Most Utah cities require a permit for driveway approaches that connect to the street, and some require permits for larger patios or any foundation work. The contractors we match you with handle permits in the cities they serve.

Stamped concrete vs. pavers — which is better?

Stamped concrete usually costs less upfront and gives a clean, continuous look; pavers handle freeze-thaw movement better and are easier to repair in sections. For Utah driveways, both work — it comes down to budget, look, and how much maintenance you want. Ask the pros you talk to what they'd recommend for your specific site.

Can concrete be poured in winter in Utah?

Yes, with precautions — heated enclosures or insulated blankets, and sometimes accelerators in the mix. But winter pours cost more and the curing conditions are less forgiving. If your project isn't urgent, spring through fall gives you the best result for the money.

How do I maintain a concrete driveway?

Seal it every 2–3 years, avoid de-icers with ammonium nitrate or ammonium sulfate (they attack concrete), shovel promptly so meltwater doesn't sit and refreeze, and fix small cracks before water gets in and widens them over winter.
