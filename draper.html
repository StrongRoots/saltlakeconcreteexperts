Concrete Work Draper Homeowners Ask For

Draper's mix of hillside lots and newer developments creates its own concrete challenges: sloped grades that need proper drainage planning, expansive soils on the benches, and homeowners who want their outdoor space to look as good as the view. Decorative and outdoor-living concrete dominates here — patios, walkways, and courtyard slabs more than basic flatwork.

  • Stamped patios — the most requested upgrade: stone and slate patterns with integral color
  • Walkways & courtyard slabs — connecting driveways, entries, and backyard spaces
  • Fire-pit & outdoor kitchen pads — level, reinforced slabs for built-in features
  • Driveways — new and replacement, graded for Draper's slopes
  • Retaining-adjacent flatwork — patios and walks tied into terraced yards

Drainage matters more on Draper's slopes

On hillside and bench lots, water management is half the job. Concrete that traps water against a foundation or sends runoff toward a neighbor creates expensive problems. Ask contractors how they're grading for drainage and where water goes — the good ones bring it up before you do.

Draper Concrete FAQs

What's the most popular patio style in Draper?

Stamped concrete in ashlar slate or flagstone patterns with earth-tone integral color — it matches Draper's upscale look at a fraction of natural stone cost. Exposed aggregate is a strong second choice for a natural, low-maintenance finish.

How do you handle drainage on a sloped Draper lot?

Proper grading (slab sloped away from the house, typically 2% minimum), channel drains where water collects, and sometimes a drain tile tie-in. This should be part of every quote on a sloped lot — if a contractor doesn't mention drainage, ask.

Can I add a concrete patio in phases?

Yes — many Draper homeowners start with the main patio and add walkways or a fire-pit pad later. A good contractor will plan control joints and edges so future phases tie in cleanly. Mention your long-term plans when getting quotes.

Do I need a permit for a patio in Draper?

Draper City requirements depend on size, location, and whether the work affects drainage or structures. Covered patios and anything structural need permits; simple flat patios often don't. Your contractor should verify with the city.

How long does stamped concrete hold its color in Utah sun?

With proper sealing every 2–3 years, integral color holds well for many years. Unsealed stamped concrete fades faster under Utah's intense sun and freeze-thaw. Budget for resealing — it's cheap insurance for a premium finish.
